# ACX100 — AI-Human Experience Framework
**by Arbora Partners (arbora.partners)**

Rate each statement on a scale of 1–5 (1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree).

## How to Use This After Every Publish

After publishing GreenElephant.org, remind Esteve to self-audit against these 8 sections. The most relevant sections for GreenElephant are:
- **Section II** — Transparency/Explainability of AI tools used on the site (Satellite Scan AI dashboard, Thesys.dev API)
- **Section III** — Accountability (who is responsible for AI assessment outcomes)
- **Section V** — Fairness/Non-Discrimination (are assessments biased toward certain communication styles?)
- **Section VI** — Technical Robustness (does the site handle AI failures gracefully?)

---

## I. Prioritize Human Agency and Oversight

- I.1. Regular training on how to oversee and intervene with AI system decisions, particularly for high-risk applications.
- I.2. "Human-in-the-loop" or "human-on-the-loop" mechanisms for critical AI outputs.
- I.3. Employees feel empowered to challenge or override AI recommendations.
- I.4. Accountability embedded at all levels with clear human decision-maker responsibility.
- I.5. A designated human monitors performance and compliance for all high-risk AI systems.
- I.6. Clear protocols for human intervention and fallback procedures if AI malfunctions.
- I.7. AI developer training includes human agency and ethical considerations.
- I.8. Organisational structure supports direct reporting channels for oversight concerns.
- I.9. User interfaces clearly indicate when a human is interacting with an AI.
- I.10. Regular audits evaluate the effectiveness of human oversight mechanisms.

## II. Foster a Culture of Transparency and Explainability

- II.1. Users are consistently informed when they interact with an AI system.
- II.2. Clear, understandable explanations for how AI systems make decisions.
- II.3. All AI-generated content is clearly labelled as such.
- II.4. AI systems have robust traceability mechanisms.
- II.5. Capabilities and limitations of AI systems are readily available to stakeholders.
- II.6. Defined process for documenting and making accessible data, algorithms, and business models.
- II.7. Internal teams trained to explain AI functionalities to external parties.
- II.8. Actively seeking feedback on the clarity of AI explanations.
- II.9. Transparency is a design requirement in the AI development lifecycle.
- II.10. Transparency reports published for significant AI deployments.

## III. Embed Accountability at All Levels

- III.1. Clear roles assigned for every AI system's development, deployment, maintenance, and oversight.
- III.2. Policies define how accountability is assigned for erroneous or harmful AI outcomes.
- III.3. Comprehensive audit trails for AI model training, data, and deployment decisions.
- III.4. Regular, independent audits of AI systems for fairness, performance, and compliance.
- III.5. Incident response plan clearly outlines steps for root cause analysis and responsibility.
- III.6. Employees educated on individual and collective responsibilities for ethical AI.
- III.7. Performance reviews include metrics related to responsible AI development.
- III.8. Stakeholders have clear channels for redress and to challenge AI outcomes.
- III.9. Procurement processes include strict vendor accountability and auditability requirements.
- III.10. Board/executive leadership regularly reviews AI system performance and risk reports.

## IV. Implement Robust Data Governance

- IV.1. Formal data governance framework for AI covering sourcing, quality, storage, and usage.
- IV.2. Processes ensure high quality, accuracy, and representativeness of training data.
- IV.3. Access to AI data strictly controlled based on legitimate need and defined roles.
- IV.4. Regularly assess and mitigate risks of data integrity issues.
- IV.5. Data processing for AI explicitly complies with GDPR and ethical guidelines.
- IV.6. Dedicated data governance plan for high-risk AI systems.
- IV.7. Mechanisms to prevent use of data obtained through unacceptable practices.
- IV.8. Data lineage thoroughly documented.
- IV.9. Employees involved in AI data management receive regular governance training.
- IV.10. Periodic audits of data governance practices for AI.

## V. Champion Fairness, Diversity, and Non-Discrimination

- V.1. Specific steps to identify, assess, and mitigate unfair biases in training data and model outputs.
- V.2. Diverse input and broad stakeholder involvement in AI design and testing.
- V.3. Accessibility requirements integrated into AI system and interface design.
- V.4. Fairness metrics and tools to monitor AI systems for discriminatory outcomes.
- V.5. Employees trained on AI bias, its impacts, and strategies for promoting fairness.
- V.6. Clear policy against AI systems that could marginalise vulnerable groups.
- V.7. Impact assessments explicitly consider social and human rights impacts.
- V.8. Data collection prioritises diverse and representative datasets.
- V.9. Mechanism for users to report perceived unfairness or discriminatory AI outcomes.
- V.10. Regular reviews ensure AI systems align with civil and human rights principles.

## VI. Ensure Technical Robustness and Safety

- VI.1. "Safety by design" principles integrated from the initial development stages.
- VI.2. All AI systems undergo rigorous testing for accuracy, reliability, and reproducibility.
- VI.3. Adversarial testing (red teaming) to identify and mitigate vulnerabilities.
- VI.4. Robust cybersecurity measures for AI systems.
- VI.5. AI systems have defined fail-safe mechanisms and fallback plans.
- VI.6. Regular monitoring of deployed AI systems for deviations, drift, and emerging safety risks.
- VI.7. Documentation includes detailed information on robustness, accuracy, and security.
- VI.8. Engineering teams receive ongoing training on secure coding and AI-specific robustness.
- VI.9. Post-incident reviews for any AI-related safety or security incidents.
- VI.10. External verification or certification obtained for high-risk AI systems.

## VII. Adopt a Risk-Based Mindset

- VII.1. Clear documented framework for classifying AI systems by risk level (EU AI Act aligned).
- VII.2. Thorough risk assessment before developing or deploying any AI system.
- VII.3. Specific risk mitigation strategies for each identified risk tier.
- VII.4. Comprehensive risk management system for all high-risk AI systems from lifecycle outset.
- VII.5. Employees understand risk tiers and corresponding compliance requirements.
- VII.6. Procurement processes evaluate vendor risk assessment and management practices.
- VII.7. Process for continuously monitoring and re-evaluating deployed AI risk profiles.
- VII.8. Decision-makers informed about risk implications of AI systems.
- VII.9. "Red line" policy for AI applications carrying unacceptable risks.
- VII.10. Regular audits verify risk classification and management processes are being followed.

## VIII. Commit to Continuous Monitoring and Adaptation

- VIII.1. Dedicated post-market monitoring system for all deployed AI systems.
- VIII.2. Actively tracks changes in global AI regulatory landscapes (EU AI Act, NIST RMF, OECD).
- VIII.3. Internal AI policies regularly reviewed and updated for new tech and regulatory developments.
- VIII.4. Dedicated resources allocated for continuous monitoring, risk detection, and adaptive governance.
- VIII.5. "Learn and adapt" culture with lessons systematically integrated into future practices.
- VIII.6. Clear process for promptly implementing changes in response to monitoring findings.
- VIII.7. Engagement with industry consortia and regulatory bodies.
- VIII.8. Incident response plans continuously refined based on monitoring insights.
- VIII.9. Employees regularly informed about the dynamic nature of AI regulation.
- VIII.10. KPIs for AI governance include metrics related to continuous monitoring effectiveness.
